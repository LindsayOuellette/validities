Group 1 · Construct validity

Did they measure the right thing?

Clue from the article

The title says “cell phone addiction.” But the questionnaire measured fear of being without a phone.

01Is fear of losing your phone the same thing as addiction?
02How else could you measure phone addiction?
  • Not quite. Someone could use their phone all day and never fear losing it.
  • Their feelings list included “over-reliance,” which isn’t a feeling, and listed “upset” twice.
  • Better: add a phone-addiction questionnaire, or record real screen time.

Group 3 · Internal validity

Can we say one thing caused another?

Clue from the article

Everyone took one survey, one time. Nothing was changed. Yet the authors wrote that phones “induce” bad feelings.

01What else could explain the link?
02Which came first: the bad feelings or the phone fear?
  • Other explanations: general anxiety, stress, loneliness, or hours of phone use (not measured).
  • We can’t tell what came first. Everything was measured at the same moment.
  • To show cause: an experiment with random assignment, or tracking people over time.
08
Group 4 · External validity

Who does this apply to?

Clue from the article

102 students, ages 18–25, at one university. Mostly women. They joined for course credit. Yet the authors wrote phone use is “problematic for everyone.”

01Who is missing from this sample?
02Who would you need to study to say “everyone”?
  • Missing: older adults, teens, people not in college, other regions and countries. Men were underrepresented.
  • Strength: the sample was racially diverse.
  • To say “everyone”: people of many ages and backgrounds, ideally chosen at random.
09

So, can we trust it?

Yes, for some claims. Not for others.
